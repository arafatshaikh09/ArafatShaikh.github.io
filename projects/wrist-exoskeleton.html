Design and development of a wearable robotic rehabilitation device integrating mechanical design, actuation and mechatronic systems.

Project Overview

Stroke and neurological injuries can significantly affect upper limb mobility, reducing an individual's ability to perform everyday tasks. This project focused on the design and development of a unilateral wrist exoskeleton aimed at supporting rehabilitation through controlled mechanical assistance.

The system combines mechanical design principles with robotic actuation and user-centred engineering considerations to create a lightweight and functional assistive device.

Engineering Objectives

  • Develop a lightweight wearable wrist exoskeleton for rehabilitation applications.
  • Design a mechanical structure capable of supporting controlled wrist movement.
  • Integrate mechanical components and actuation systems.
  • Evaluate structural performance and improve the design through engineering analysis.
  • Consider ergonomics, comfort and practical usability.

Design & Development

CAD Modelling

Detailed 3D CAD models were developed to define the mechanical structure of the exoskeleton. The design process considered component interaction, assembly requirements and manufacturability.

Analysis & Validation

Engineering analysis was performed to evaluate structural performance, identify potential design limitations and improve reliability of the system.
